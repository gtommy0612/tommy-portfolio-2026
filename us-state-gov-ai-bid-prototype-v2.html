02 The key insight

Evaluators trust what they can click through.

A slide says we understand the workflow. A working prototype proves it. So the goal became a clickable product that stays faithful to the agency's business logic, not a prettier deck.

03 Key decisions

Decision 1: Let the agency's own documents do the discovery

Traditional discovery wasn't possible in 48 hours, but the business rules were already written down. I fed the government business documents into Claude to extract the rules, then had it generate UI grounded in that logic. I mapped flows for every user role with clear data perimeters and access permissions.

Trade-off

AI can misread a rule, so extracted rules went through a verification step before becoming screens. [TODO: Tommy · how you verified, and other options you ruled out]

Decision 2: Ship clickable HTML, built on tokens, not static screens

A clickable prototype was the proof, so speed of building screens became the bottleneck. I structured design tokens in Figma Variables on top of the design system, so the AI-generated flows scaled into consistent high-fidelity screens, then delivered the result as an interactive HTML prototype within the 48-hour window.

Trade-off

Depth vs. breadth. I designed the complete happy path for the core flows and deliberately set aside edge cases, such as an applicant cancelling mid-registration.

Decision 3: After the win, make the tokens engineering's tokens

Winning the bid was the start, not the end. As Product Designer and PM for the implementation cycle, I designed the edge cases the bid had skipped and aligned Figma Variables with engineering's variable naming conventions. Developers built from pre-validated blocks, and the tokens were reused across later bids.

05 Reflection

Senior work here meant choosing what not to design. Under a 48-hour window, prioritising the happy path over edge cases is what made the deadline possible.

Align with BD earlier

Set up direct data-sharing with the business development team from day one, so the whole team shares one understanding of complex client workflows.

Validate with users, not assumptions

Move from relying on past project experience to continuous user feedback, so system logic is checked against real behaviour.

Next: data-driven iteration

Usability testing with key personas, plus GA and heatmaps, to combine qualitative and quantitative insight.
