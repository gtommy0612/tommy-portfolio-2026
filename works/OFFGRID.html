Project Background

FSS (Firearm Safety System) is an end-to-end US state-level firearm safety management system that connects Applicants, Sheriffs, Instructors, State Admin Users and Firearm Dealers. This project aimed to deliver a highly secure and compliant multi-platform system proposal by rapidly refactoring complex regulatory business logic.

01. Situation & Task

Our company faced a critical, high-stakes opportunity to bid on a US state government contract. However, we were given an extremely tight 48-hour deadline to submit a comprehensive proposal. The challenge required quickly mastering unfamiliar firearm safety regulations and mapping out complex business logic that involved multiple interconnected user roles.

Business Goal

Conceptualize, design, and deliver a fully interactive, high-fidelity portal prototype within the 48-hour sprint to win the $1.6M+ government tender.

Design Goal

Synthesize fragmented regulatory requirements into a unified, clear Information Architecture (IA) and cross-platform UI flow that demonstrates both security and seamless usability.

02. Action & Decisions

Phase 1: The 48-Hour Sprint — AI-Augmented Information Architecture

To overcome the severe time constraint, I pioneered an AI-driven UX workflow:

  1. AI-Powered Synthesis: I fed hundreds of pages of raw state regulations and RFP requirements into NotebookLM. By leveraging semantic extraction, I instantly summarized core business rules and mapped out the initial Information Architecture (IA).
  2. Multi-Role Flow Harmonization: Based on the AI-generated architecture, I quickly mapped out the UI flows for all 4 key personas (Applicants, Police, Instructors, and Vendors), defining clear data perimeters and access permissions.

04. Reflection & Next Steps

Under a critical 48-hour window, the ultimate trade-off was "depth vs. breadth." I strategically prioritized designing the complete Happy Path (the primary core user flows) while deliberately sidelining edge cases (e.g., an applicant cancelling mid-registration). This project taught me that senior leadership means knowing how to make pragmatic design compromises to meet pressing business milestones.

What I Would Do Differently

Earlier Cross-Functional Alignment

Establish direct data-sharing channels with the business development team from day one to ensure a unified, holistic understanding of complex client workflows across the entire team.

User-Centric Validation over Assumptions

Shift from relying on past project experiences to advocating for continuous user feedback loops, ensuring system logic is validated by real user behaviors rather than assumptions.

Next Step

Data-Driven Iteration

Launch targeted usability testing with key personas while implementing GA and Heatmaps to merge qualitative and quantitative insights for continuous product optimization.
