Design highlights

01 Challenge

A first contract with a new state agency, and a way into its other departments. I heard about the bid with the deadline already close. The agency sent PDFs and asked for a design proposal. Nobody on the team had worked in this domain.

Time

48 hours to submit. Our last comparable bid took 5 days of concept work.

Five parties, in order

An applicant passes a sheriff's check, then a required course, then buys from a dealer, while the agency oversees and reviews.

No discovery

No interviews, no process audit. The business rules existed only in the documents.

02 The approach

Make the proposal the first draft of the product.

A design proposal usually stops mattering once the bid is decided. I wanted ours to keep working: a file the agency could click through to see what we understood, and the starting point we would build from if we won.

03 Key decisions

Decision 1: Map how the five parties connect before drawing any screen

Problem
Five parties act in a fixed order, and the rules for that order existed only in PDFs.
I chose
Lay out how the roles relate and the applicant's full path first, then split it into each role's screens.
Instead of
Drawing each role's screens straight from the documents.
Why
One step depends on another. After the sheriff approves, the applicant takes a basic or an extended course, depending on the training history they gave when they applied.

Decision 2: Send clickable HTML, not static Figma screens

Problem
A design proposal for a domain we had just met, due in 48 hours.
I chose
A clickable HTML prototype built with Claude Code, on tokens generated from our existing design system.
Instead of
Static Figma screens. Our last comparable bid took 5 days that way, and we lost it.
Why
A working file shows the client what we understood, and it is something we can build from if we win. The company also wanted to adopt an AI development workflow.
Trade-off
Happy path only, for every outside role from applicant to dealer. Left out: the sheriff withdrawing an approval, the agency's review of training records, reports and settings. I agreed that scope with our US PM and leadership before building.

Decision 3: Give the applicant one page that answers "where am I?"

Problem
An applicant waits on three offices they never see.
I chose
Make the application itself the map: review results, course records, downloadable proof and the next step on one page.
Why
Wherever the application is, the applicant can see who has it and what they need to do next.

04 What the prototype got wrong

I assumed a reviewed application locks. It seemed safe: the training history was part of the application, and the sheriff had already approved it.

When we started building, the agency told us otherwise. Personal details lock after review, but the training section stays editable. In the interface the fix was small, a change in which fields are read-only. But it was exactly the kind of rule I couldn't confirm in 48 hours, and the prototype gave the agency something concrete to correct.

05 Result

Won, and shipped

The $1.6M+ contract was awarded. After 11 weeks of design and build, the portal is live.

How −60% was measured

Against our last bid of similar size: 5 days of Figma concept work then, 2 days here.

Built from the same tokens

Engineers built from the tokens behind the prototype, so the bid's screens became the starting point for delivery.

06 Reflection

Speed moved the risk. It didn't remove it. AI got me from documents to a working prototype in two days, but some of the rules in it were guesses, and nobody could tell which. The training-record rule was one of them.

Next time I'd send an assumption list with the prototype: the rules we weren't sure of, written as questions for the agency. Our guesses would become questions for the client before kickoff, not surprises during the build.
